Caissa School of Chess – Curriculum Goal & Outcome

Goal: Build strong basics + understanding of rules

Beginner Level (24 sessions)

  Learn how each piece moves and basic rules (check, checkmate, castling, en passant,

promotion)

Identify hanging pieces and safe captures

  Understand attack, defense (ABCD), and captures

  Learn checkmates in 1 move and simple mating ideas

  Understand draw rules
  Build habit of thinking before moving

Introduction to openings (basic principles)

 Outcome: Independently play a full game following all rules, consistently identify hanging pieces,
and spot simple 1-move tactics (checkmates, captures, checks).

Foundation Level (96 sessions)

 Foundation 1

Goal: Introduction to tactics + basic calculation

  Learn key tactics: pin, fork, skewer, discovered attack
  Recognize mate threats & simple checkmates (1–2 moves)
  Learn opening traps and mistakes
  Start thinking about defense and punishment

Outcome: Regularly spot basic tactics (pin, fork, skewer) in games and solve simple 1–2 move
combinations, while avoiding common beginner mistakes.

Foundation 2

Goal: Deeper tactical understanding + pawn endgames

  Advanced tactics: overloading, deflection, x-ray, intermediate moves
  Learn king & pawn endgames (rule of square, opposition)
  Study checkmate patterns (important memory building)
  Exposure to forced moves and combinations

 Outcome: Calculate short sequences (2–3 moves), recognize common checkmate patterns, and
confidently play basic king & pawn endgames.

 Foundation 3

Goal: Thinking deeper (3–4 moves) + real game exposure

  Solve mate in 3 & 4 problems
  Learn attacking ideas on f7, g7, h7 (weak squares)
  Analyze own games

  Learn stalemate & drawing techniques

Outcome: Calculate 3–4 move combinations, identify attacking opportunities on weak squares
(f7/g7/h7), and analyze their own games with guidance.

 Foundation 4

Goal: Structured thinking & planning

  Learn blunder-check routine
  Build attack plans & identify targets


  Learn calculation and conversion of advantage

Improve decision-making (compare moves, eliminate bad ones)
Introduction to endgame theory (Lucena, Philidor)

Outcome: Follow a clear thinking process (blunder check + candidate moves), creates basic attack
plans, and converts simple winning positions into victories.

Intermediate Level (96 sessions)

Intermediate 1

Goal: Introduction to positional play + structured calculation

  Learn how to calculate properly
  Concepts like initiative, rook activity, pawn breaks
  Study famous players (Anand, Kasparov, Karpov)
  Understand opening structures (Italian, Scotch)

Outcome: Apply structured calculation (checks, captures, threats), understand basic positional
concepts, and play common opening structures with a plan.

Intermediate 2

Goal: Master piece coordination & endgames

  Learn piece activity, king safety, space advantage
  Study rook & queen endgames
  Understand pawn structures (Caro-Kann, Sicilian)


Improve intuition and evaluation

Outcome: Demonstrate improved piece coordination, evaluate positions using king safety and
pawn structure, and handle practical rook/queen endgames.

Intermediate 3

Goal: Advanced pawn structures & transitions

  Learn isolated, doubled, backward pawns
  Master endgames (Lucena, Philidor deeper)

  Understand transition: middlegame → endgame
  Study legends like Carlsen, Botvinnik

Outcome: Understand pawn structures (isolated, doubled, backward), transition confidently into
endgames, and apply key theoretical positions (Lucena/Philidor).

Intermediate 4

Goal: High-level strategy & practical play

  Learn attack on king (castled & uncastled)
  Handle equal/worse positions
  Study fortress, counterplay, sacrifices
  Master decision-making under pressure

 Outcome: Build and execute strategic plans, handle equal or worse positions practically, and
demonstrate tournament-level decision-making and resilience.

Final Summary:

 Beginner: Plays legal games + spots simple tactics
 Foundation: Calculates combinations + builds thinking process
 Intermediate: Plans, evaluates, and competes like a tournament player
